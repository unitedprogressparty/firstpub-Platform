### [2025-02-01] US Strike in Somalia (Bosaso/Golis Mountains): ISIS - 14 to 46 killed

- **Summary:** US military strike in Bosaso/Golis Mountains, Somalia targeting ISIS. Total casualties: 14-46. Civilian casualties: 0-0. Description: On February 1, 2025, the United States conducted air strikes against ISIS in Somalia. Defense officials told media outlets that the strikes were carried out by fighter jets launched from the USS Harry S. Truman, which was based in the Red Sea. President Trump announced the strikes via a post on his social media platform Truth Social on February 1, 2025, stating, “This morning I ordered precision Military air strikes on the Senior ISIS Attack Planner and other terrorists he recruited and led in Somalia.” 
It is not clear how many strikes were conducted or their exact locations. 

Various statements from American officials referred to airstrikes in the plural including Trump’s Truth Social post, a statement by Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, and the initial press release from AFRICOM. However, on March 5, 2025, AFRICOM told New America as part of a response to a query that the operation is “considered 1 airstrike as part of a major operation targeting ISIS Somalia (multiple aircraft/ munitions involved).” On February 11, 2025, AFRICOM provided an update saying that the strikes occurred about 50 miles southeast of Bosasso, targeting a “series of cave complexes,” but did not specify how many strikes were conducted. The statement by Hegseth placed the strikes in the Golis Mountains. Voice of America reported that General Adan Abdi Hashi, commander of the Puntland Devish Forces, said “The strikes targeted about 10 locations in the Cal-Miskaad areas, most of them caves, and we believe that many of the militants were killed.” Residents in the Bari region’s town of Qandala told Voice of America that they saw plumes of smoke from the strikes and heard at least seven large explosions. Garowe Online reported that “multiple sources” told them that “approximately six airstrikes hit various locations in the Al-Miskaad mountains.” Garowe Online cited reports of “significant impact” in four distinct particular areas: Qurac, Buqo, Wangable, and Dhasaan. AFP quoted Abdirahman Adan, a member of the army near Bosasso, as reporting hearing at least five explosions. Senior Director for Counterterrorism at the NSC Sebastian Gorka appeared to confirm the at least six number, telling Breitbart News that “This wasn’t one cave with one individual,” adding, “This was—I’m not going to go into exact numbers but this was more than half a dozen caves that were being exploited by ISIS to hide in and that they thought they were safe in.” On February 3rd, Trump released video of the strikes via his social media. Garowe Online reported that analysis of the video showed seven distinct strikes. 

New America previously listed this entry as consisting of seven strikes, but as of March 5th is listing it as a single strike based on the AFRICOM statement that it was a single strike involving multiple munitions and aircraft. The prior New America count of seven strikes was based on Garowe Online’s reporting on the strikes and analysis of the video and Voice of America’s interviews with residents of Qandala (who referenced seven large explosions). New America seeks to count separate strikes where engagements are distinguished in location or timing. The count of strikes may update if further information is forthcoming.

AFRICOM’s initial assessment was that the strike killed “multiple ISIS-Somalia operatives” and did not kill any civilians, but AFRICOM did not initially provide an assessment of the total number of casualties or further specifics regarding the number or identity of the militants killed. The AFRICOM press release stated, “U.S. Africa Command will continue to assess the results of this operation and will provide additional information as appropriate. Specific details about the operation will not be released in order to ensure continued operations security.” AFRICOM’s February 11th update assessed that approximately 14 ISIS-Somalia operatives were killed.” Trump’s Truth Social post stated referring to the ISIS militants the strikes targeted, “The strikes destroyed the caves they live in, and killed many terrorists without, in any way, harming civilians.” Hegseth’s statement similarly stated, “Our initial assessment is that multiple operatives were killed in the airstrikes and no civilians were harmed.” AFP reported that Mohamed Ali, a military commander in the Bossaso area, told them, "We don't know so far the number of the casualties, but we believe the missiles precisely hit the targets." A statement from Puntland officials thanked the U.S. for the strikes, saying that they “resulted in the elimination of several high-ranking #ISIS members.” Puntland’s government also thanked the United Arab Emirates alongside the U.S. “for their participation in the airstrikes conducted as part of the ongoing operation to eliminate #ISIS (Daesh) terrorists from the #CalMiskaad mountains in the Bari region.” Garowe Online and Hiraan Online reported that the general leading Puntland's operation against ISIS, put the strikes' toll at over 46 militants, saying that it also killed senior figures but not identifying them. 

The strikes appear to have targeted –among others – a senior ISIS planner. In its February 11th update, AFRICOM stated that “Among those killed was Ahmed Maeleninine, a key ISIS recruiter, financier, and external operations leader responsible for the deployment of jihadists into the United States and across Europe.” Voice of America cited Puntland state minister for presidency, Abdifitah Mohamed Abdinur as describing Maeleninine as “an Omani-born man in his 40s who was wanted for international crimes and his movements and activities have been followed at least for the last two years, as he was hiding in the mountainous area of Puntland.” Abdinur added, “He was a thorn removed from the flesh of Somalis and the world population, and he eventually tasted what he deserved.” In his Truth Social post, Trump asserted that “Our Military has targeted this ISIS Attack Planner for years,” but claimed that President Biden’s administration had not acted “quickly enough to get the job done.” The initial AFRICOM press release did not specify that the strikes targeted a specific operative, but was titled “U.S. Forces Conduct Strike Targeting ISIS-Somalia.” Hegseth’s statement said that the strikes were “targeting ISIS-Somalia operatives in the Golis mountains.” At least under the standard rules applied under the Biden administration, the targeted nature of the strike and the apparent presidential approval would signal that at least one target was a senior leader, as the New York Times writes. On February 3rd, Garowe Online reported that one of the caves that was targeted was believed to be the hideout of Abdulqadir Mumin, the leader of ISIS's Somalia branch who the U.S. previously targeted in a strike under Biden, and of Abdirahman Fahiye, described as a senior commander. On February 7, 2025, the British Tabloid the Daily Mail quoted Sebastain Gorka, Senior Director for Counterterrorism at the NSC, on the operation. Gorka named the target that Trump referenced as Ahmed Maeleminine, telling the tabloid, "This man has recruited terrorists from around the world, trained them to kill innocents, and deployed them back into the West to execute Jihadi attacks in more than one nation." Gorka explicitly referenced Maeleminine as the individual Trump referenced in his social media post, claiming, "As President Trump pointed out in his viral post on the operation, our people had been tracking and surveilling Maeleminine for a very long time but the Biden Administration repeatedly refused to take any action against this Jihadi mastermind." An anonymous intelligence official cited by the Washington Post, “said the strike targeted a cell responsible for planning external attacks, including against American interests and their allies.” 

The New York Times identified doubt within the Defense Department that the strikes responded to a particularly grave threat, reporting, “The strikes were more symbolic than substantive, several U.S. military and defense officials said, meant more to burnish Mr. Trump’s image as a commander in chief protecting the country from terrorists in the early days of his administration than to neutralize a serious threat.” 

According to the Washington Post, the February 1st strike came amid a larger operation by Puntland forces, and that operation had been “planned for months and launched on Jan. 2.” Puntland officials told the Post that they had sought support from AFRICOM, but efforts had been “hamstrung” by the transition. The Post also reported that “An Africom official said the Pentagon was monitoring the operation but not providing any support. Puntland says it also gets no help from the Somali state, which is Africom’s main point of contact.”
